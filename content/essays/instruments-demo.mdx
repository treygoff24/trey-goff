This piece is a bench, not an argument. Every claim in it is invented, every figure is
made up, and every source points at a placeholder domain. It exists so the audit grammar
has something to be wrong about.

## What the bench measures

The bench runs a fictional transit authority in a city that does not exist. Ridership on
its four lines has been falling for a decade, and the authority has spent that decade
disagreeing with itself about why.

The headline number everyone repeats is <instrument-stat data-id="ridership-drop"></instrument-stat>,
which is the figure the authority prints on the front of its annual report. I went looking
for the arithmetic behind it and could not reconstruct it from the tables in the same
report. The number is probably close to right and is certainly not derived the way the
report implies.

Two things follow from that, and only one of them is a finding. The first is that the
decline is real: every series the authority publishes points the same direction, and so
does the farebox data it does not publish. The second is that the authority does not know
why, which I believe but cannot show.

## Where the argument thins out

The most common explanation is that riders left for cars during the closures and never
came back. I find this plausible and I want to be clear that plausible is all it is. The
survey the explanation rests on had a response rate low enough that the authority
declined to weight it.

There is a version of this story where the closures had almost no effect at all. Two of
the four lines lost riders at the same rate through the closure years as they had for the
five years before, which is the single strongest piece of evidence against everything I
have argued here. I would rather state it than have it pointed out to me.

I was also offered a set of internal complaint records by someone who should not have had
them. I have not read them and will not describe what I was told they contain.

## What would settle it

The authority is scheduled to publish its reconstructed series in 2027. If the
reconstruction shows the closure years as a break, the common explanation survives. If it
shows the same slope through the closures, it does not.

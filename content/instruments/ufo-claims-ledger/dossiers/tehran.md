# Finding: 1976 Tehran incident & DSP satellites (claims C246–C253)

Research date: 2026-07-21. Lanes: exa-agent (primary), direct curl fetches of primary documents, Wayback Machine for one dead source. All quotes below were fetched and verified verbatim at the cited URL on 2026-07-21.

## What was claimed (podcast, section P)

- **C246** — Derakhshani wants DSP (Defense Support Program) satellite records; DSP has tracked "fast walkers/slow walkers" for decades. (Type A)
- **C247** — DSP satellites cover outer-Earth orbit and serve as a first line of detection for objects entering Earth's atmosphere. (Type C)
- **C248** — Aerospace researchers Lee Graham and "Ron Reiger" [= Ron Regehr; dictation/transcription near-miss] claim to have analyzed DSP printout records from 1976/77 at the date/time of the Tehran UFO "dogfight." (Type A)
- **C249** — Graham and Regehr claim a DSP satellite detected an infrared/heat anomaly over Tehran at the moment of the event. (Type A)
- **C250** — In a James Fox film, a pilot from the Tehran incident says he **shot at** the UFO. (Type A)
- **C251** — The pilot says he then lost control of his aircraft due to "some electromagnetic outage" that "just shut down" the aircraft. (Type A)
- **C252** — The DSP records related to this incident are still classified; the public cannot independently access them. (Type B)
- **C253** — Derakhshani wants DSP records to cross-correlate with STS-48/STS-80 timing. (Type A)

## What the primary sources show

### 1. The DIA document is real, and the "outstanding report" evaluation form is real

The core document is a 3-page DoD Joint Chiefs of Staff Message Center teletype (sent 19–23 Sep 1976, distribution including White House, SecState, CIA, NSA, DIA), released under FOIA on 31 August 1977 to Charles Huffer, re-released by DIA to John Greenewald in 1996. I downloaded the DIA FOIA release scan (6 pp: DIA cover letter U-80,597/PO/FOIA dated 22 August 1996, signed Robert P. Richardson, Chief, FOIA Staff, plus the teletype) from https://theblackvault.com/documents/ufos/1976iranincident.pdf — verified real PDF, page images inspected; page 4 of the cable reads verbatim (teletype caps):

> "LIKE LIGHTENING. THE AIRCRAFT AND AREA WHERE THE OBJECT IS BELIEVED TO HAVE LANDED ARE BEING CHECKED FOR POSSIBLE RADIATION. RO COMMENTS: [redacted] ACTUAL INFORMATION CONTAINED IN THIS REPORT WAS OBTAINED FROM SOURCE IN CONVERSATION WITH A SUB-SOURCE, AND IIAF PILOT OF ONE OF THE F-4S. MORE INFORMATION WILL BE FORWARDED WHEN IT BECOMES AVAILABLE."

Saved locally: `sources/tehran-1976/dia-foia-1976-iran-incident-blackvault.pdf` (retrieved 2026-07-21). NSA also hosts copies (https://www.nsa.gov/portals/75/documents/news-features/declassified-documents/ufo/us_gov_iran_case.pdf — NSA's WAF blocked my direct download but the URL is live in their declassified-UFO index).

Key cable content (full transcription verified at NICAP, https://www.nicap.org/reports/760919tehran_rep.htm):

> "As the F-4 approached a range of 25 NM, he lost all instrumentation and communications (UHF and intercom). He broke off the intercept and headed back to Shahrokhi. When the F-4 turned away from the object and apparently was no longer a threat to it, the aircraft regained all instrumentation and communications."

> "The pilot attempted to fire an AIM-9 missile at the object but at that instant his weapons control panel went off and he lost all communications (UHF and interphone). At this point the pilot initiated a turn and negative G dive to get away."

Attached to the cable was a DIA "Defense Information Report Evaluation" form (IR No. 6 846 0139 76, prepared by Maj. Roland B. Evans, USAF). Boxes checked: reliability "Confirmed by other sources"; value "High (Unique, Timely, and of Major Significance)"; utility "Potentially Useful." Remarks section, verbatim (verified in the FUFOR case summary, https://www.nicap.org/reports/760919fufor_docs.pdf, saved as `sources/tehran-1976/fufor-case-summary-nicap.pdf`, and in Fawcett & Greenwood, *Clear Intent*, pp. 82–85 as transcribed at https://www.nicap.org/reports/760919tehran_rep.htm):

> "An outstanding report. This case is a classic which meets all the criteria necessary for a valid study of the UFO phenomenon:
> a) The object was seen by multiple witnesses from different locations (i.e., Shamiran [sic], Mehrabad, and the dry lake bed) and viewpoints (both airborne and from the ground).
> b) The credibility of many of the witnesses was high (an Air Force general, qualified aircrews, and experienced tower operators).
> c) Visual sightings were confirmed by radar.
> d) Similar electromagnetic effects (EME) were reported by three separate aircraft.
> e) There were physiological effects on some crew members (i.e., loss of night vision due to the brightness of the object).
> f) An inordinate amount of maneuverability was displayed by the UFOs."

Note what the evaluation form is: an intelligence-report quality grade by one analyst (Evans), not a scientific analysis or an official USG conclusion that the object was anomalous. Per FUFOR, Richard Hall tracked Evans down in 1997 and Evans expressed willingness to testify to Congress about the case.

A second government document, "Now You See It, Now You Don't," was a classified narrative released in 1981 under FOIA — but per Brian Dunning it was "an editorial in the typed, mimeographed newsletter of the United States Air Force Security Services quarterly MIJI newsletter," a dramatized retelling of the same information in Lt. Col. Olin Mooy's debriefing memo, not an independent analysis (https://skeptoid.com/episodes/315).

### 2. The pilot testimony (Jafari)

Maj. (later Gen.) Parviz Jafari flew the second F-4. He testified publicly at the 12 November 2007 National Press Club event organized with James Fox and Leslie Kean, and appears in Fox's documentary *I Know What I Saw* (2009) — the podcast's guess of "Out of the Blue" is close but the Tehran interview footage is from the later film. His 2007 statement, verbatim (verified at https://medium.com/on-the-trail-of-the-saucers/the-tehran-ufo-incident-and-the-iranian-fighter-pilot-who-chased-it-e863d913de8d; matching text in Kean, *UFOs: Generals, Pilots, and Government Officials Go on the Record*, p. 88, excerpted at https://ufoquotes.com/2010/01/parviz-jafari/):

> "Four other objects, with different shapes, separated from the main one at different times during the close encounter. Whenever they were close to me, my radar jammed and my radio communications were garbled. One of the objects headed toward me. I thought it was a missile. **I tried to launch a heat seeker missile at it, but my missile panel went out.**"

And (Kean p. 88 version):

> "I attempted to fire, and looked at the panel to confirm my selection of the missile. Suddenly, nothing was working. The weapons control panel was out, and I lost all the instruments, and the radio."

So the consistent record — 1976 DIA cable and Jafari's own 2007 testimony — is that he **attempted** to fire and the weapons panel died before launch. No shot was fired. He also reported his ejection-seat indicator malfunctioning and that he escaped with a negative-G dive; he did not say the aircraft (engines/flight controls) "shut down," and he retained enough control to dive, evade, orbit Mehrabad, and land.

### 3. The DSP claim (Graham/Regehr)

DSP is real and its role is documented: geosynchronous infrared early-warning satellites detecting missile launches, space launches, and nuclear detonations (USSF fact sheet: https://www.spaceforce.mil/About-Us/Fact-Sheets/Fact-Sheet-Display/Article/2197774/defense-support-program-satellites/). "Fast walker" as NORAD-lingo for anomalous DSP tracks entered the literature via a leaked printout supplied by Regehr, Graham, and Joe Stefula to *UFO Magazine* (publisher Don Ecker's account: https://www.theparacast.com/forum/threads/lee-graham-ron-regehr-and-dsp-satellite.5622/ — "Both Ron and Lee worked on the DSP satellite array... Both Lee and Ron were responsible (along with Joseph Steffula) in providing me with information that we broke in UFO Magazine on FASTWALKERs"). Regehr (retired Aerojet engineer) lectured on "The Iranian UFO Encounter" at the 2006 MUFON Symposium (https://realtvufos.blogspot.com/2015/12/ufo-lecture-mufon-symposium-denver-2006.html) and discussed DSP on Art Bell, 1 May 1998 (https://artbellarchive.org/episode/may-1-1998-spy-satellites-ron-regehr-and-derrel-sims). So C248/C249 are accurately reported *as claims made*.

The claimed detection itself does not survive scrutiny. Brad Sparks — who says he discovered the leaked DSP printout — wrote (NICAP case directory, http://www.nicap.org/760919tehran_dir.htm):

> "The leaked DSP satellite data printout that I discovered has the exact times of events to the second -- and the entry here is not at the time of the Iranian jet case--- so it has nothing to do with it, is not confirmation of the incident."

Sparks's detailed 1999 critique (Jerry Cohen's page, original domain now hijacked by a gambling site; archived at http://web.archive.org/web/20160207221610/http://www.cohenufo.org:80/dsp_claims.html):

> "there is not sufficient information to say that the DSP actually detected the UFO/UFO's, possibly it detected the F-4's instead, or maybe it was an oil burnoff which is prevalent in the Middle East. The printout catalog of DSP anomalies you depict... actually gives only the DAY not the exact TIME and in unclassified presentation there is NO LOCATION apparent."

> "this one is an 'SR' which we do not have a definition or meaning for... For all we know the IR source was on the other side of the world."

> "the old DSP satellites scanned the earth once every 10 seconds. So 238 scans equals 2,380 seconds or about 39.7 minutes NOT 'more than an hour.'"

> "Even the date is a problem because the DSP data lists it as 18th not 19th... [and] there is the problem of why only ONE DSP satellite seems to have detected this IR source. By 1976 there were several DSP satellites covering the Eurasian mainland... More than one DSP should have detected this IR source if it was a real source."

Jan Aldrich (Project 1947), same page: "How do we [know] that the DSP signature is in fact the UFO and not a gas flare on any oil field... Show me proof, and I'll look at it." (He also attacks Graham's reliability directly.)

No official DSP data on this event has ever been released; the only artifact is the leaked, unclassified-presentation printout of contested date/time/location.

### 4. The skeptical case (Klass, Oberg, Dunning)

Philip J. Klass (*UFOs: The Public Deceived*, 1983) argued the initial light was an astronomical body, probably Jupiter (James Oberg concurring), plus pilot error and known equipment failure. Per the Wikipedia summary of Klass and Bridgstock (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1976_Tehran_UFO_incident) and Dunning's independent write-up (https://skeptoid.com/episodes/315):

- Dunning, from Klass's contractor interviews: "The Westinghouse tech at Shahrokhi confirmed that only the second F-4 was reported to have experienced any electrical problems during the flight; the first F-4 was never sent in for maintenance. The McDonnell Douglas tech at Shahrokhi noted that the second F-4 had a long history of intermittent electrical outages that the IIAF had never been able to fix... Both techs stated that the Shahrokhi base was notorious for low quality work and poor record keeping."
- On Jupiter: Dunning notes Jupiter was in the sky where the pilots looked once on station, and witnesses described the object as "similar to a star but much brighter."
- On the falling objects: 19 September was near the peak of the Gamma/Southern Piscids meteor showers.
- On the beeper: "Col. Mooy noted that the beeping transponder appeared to be from an American C-141" and that crews "had been having problems with the beepers being ejected simply by turbulence over the mountains just north of Tehran" (verified at skeptoid.com/episodes/315).
- Dunning's conclusion, verbatim: "Once we look at all the story's elements without the presumption of an alien spaceship, the only thing unusual about the Tehran 1976 UFO case is that planes were chasing celestial objects and had equipment failures. There have been many cases where planes had equipment failures, and there have been many cases where planes misidentified celestial objects. Once in a while, both will happen on the same flight."

Counter-critique exists: Martin Shough's NARCAP paper responding to Klass (image-only scan, no text layer; saved as `sources/tehran-1976/narcap-shough-klass-critique.pdf`, retrieved 2026-07-21 from https://www.nicap.org/reports/NARCAP_Iran_Klass.pdf), and Bruce Maccabee's analysis (linked from the NICAP directory) arguing the radar lock and correlated EM effects on two aircraft plus an airliner resist the Jupiter/equipment story.

## Provenance chains

- **DIA cable + evaluation form:** JCS teletype (19–23 Sep 1976) → leak of the evaluation to NICAP (Nov 1976 UFO Investigator) → Huffer FOIA release 31 Aug 1977 → Fawcett/Greenwood *Clear Intent* (1984) → 1996 DIA re-release to Greenewald (scan in hand). Root source for the narrative inside the cable: one IIAF pilot (Jafari) via a source/sub-source chain, as the cable itself states ("obtained from source in conversation with a sub-source, and IIAF pilot of one of the F-4s"). The cable is a real document (Type B: confirmed); its contents are a secondhand debriefing of participant testimony, not sensor data.
- **Jafari testimony:** consistent from the 1976 debrief (Mooy memo) through Sightings (1994), NPC statement (2007), Kean book (2010), Fox film (2009). Single-witness root for the dogfight details (backseater Damirian corroborates; first-jet pilot Nazeri's account surfaced only in 1994).
- **DSP detection claim:** a single leaked, unclassified DSP anomaly-catalog printout → Regehr/Graham interpretation (1990s lectures/radio, MUFON 2006) → podcast. No official release; the two named UFO researchers who examined the printout most closely (Sparks, Aldrich) both reject the Tehran linkage. This is a textbook single-uncorroborated-root citation circle.

## Steelman both directions

**Best case that it's genuinely anomalous:** Multiple independent witness classes (civilians, tower controller Pirouzi, Gen. Youssefi, two aircrews) over ~4 hours; an airborne radar lock with 707-tanker-class return; EM effects reported on two F-4s and a civil airliner, each correlated with proximity/azimuth and restored on withdrawal; weapons-panel failure at the exact moment of attempted launch; a DIA analyst graded it "High (Unique, Timely, and of Major Significance)"; the case's author was still willing to testify decades later; and Klass never interviewed any Iranian participant — his equipment-failure account rests on two American contractors' recollections and contradicts the cable written within days (which says the *first* F-4 lost instruments and comms).

**Best mundane explanation:** The anchor light was very likely Jupiter (position matches once the F-4s were on station; "star but brighter" descriptions); the date sits on the Piscids meteor peaks, explaining the "missile" and the descending object; Jafari's specific F-4 had a documented history of intermittent electrical failures out of a maintenance shop with a poor reputation; radar lock can be a false/manual-track artifact; the beeper traced to a C-141 transponder known to eject in local turbulence; the impressive "classified documents" are one secondhand debrief cable, a quality-grade form filled in by a single analyst, and a security-service newsletter editorial. The DSP "confirmation" evaporates on inspection (wrong/uncertain date, no time or location in the unclassified printout, undefined "SR" tag, single-satellite detection where several should have seen a real source).

**Where it lands:** the incident is a genuinely well-documented *report* — the strongest thing about Tehran 1976 is the paper trail, not the sensor data. Every extraordinary element roots back to eyewitness testimony from a startled nighttime intercept; the one claimed instrument-record corroboration (DSP) fails. The case remains formally unexplained but has a coherent, multi-part mundane account.

## Verdicts

- **C246** (Derakhshani wants DSP records; DSP tracked fast/slow walkers) — **confirmed** as a statement of intent (Type A, from transcript). The embedded factual rider is partially supported: "fast walker" is real leaked-DSP terminology (Ecker/UFO Magazine via Regehr, Graham, Stefula), but "tracked for decades" as applied to UFOs rests on a handful of leaked, contested printouts. Would change with: official DSP anomaly-catalog releases.
- **C247** (DSP as first line of detection) — **likely, with correction.** DSP satellites are geosynchronous IR early-warning platforms staring down at Earth for missile/space launches and nuclear detonations (USSF fact sheet); they can detect bright IR events including reentries. "Cover outer-Earth orbit" misdescribes the geometry — they look down from GEO; they are not an orbit-surveillance fence.
- **C248** (Graham & Regehr analyzed 1976/77 DSP printouts for Tehran) — **confirmed as Type A** (they did make this claim and presented analysis: MUFON 2006 lecture, 1994/1998 radio appearances). Note: podcast's "Ron Reiger" = Ron Regehr.
- **C249** (DSP detected an IR anomaly over Tehran at the moment of the event) — **unsupported**, leaning debunked. The only artifact is a leaked unclassified printout whose entry lists the wrong/ambiguous date (18th), no time-of-day, no location, an undefined "SR" tag, and a single-satellite detection where multiple DSP birds covered the region. Sparks (who found the printout): "it has nothing to do with it, is not confirmation of the incident." Would change with: declassified DSP data with time/geolocation matching 0130–0230 local, 19 Sep 1976, Tehran.
- **C250** (pilot said he **shot at** the UFO in a James Fox film) — **contested - audit flagged.** Jafari appears in Fox's *I Know What I Saw* (2009), not *Out of the Blue*, and his consistent testimony (1976 cable; 2007 NPC) is that he **tried** to fire an AIM-9 and the weapons panel went dead before launch. No weapon was fired. [Audit note: the Medium URL cited for the "heat seeker missile" quote is a member-only paywalled story and could not be re-verified; the substantive conclusion stands on the two anchors that did re-verify (DIA cable at NICAP; Kean p. 88 at ufoquotes.com). See audit section.]
- **C251** (lost control amid an EM outage that "shut down" the aircraft) — **likely as paraphrase, overstated in detail.** Jafari reported losing weapons panel, instruments, and radio (and an ejection-seat malfunction indication) — but the aircraft kept flying; he executed a deliberate negative-G evasive dive and landed. "Instruments/comms/weapons failed" is supported testimony; "aircraft shut down / lost control" is not.
- **C252** (DSP records still classified/publicly inaccessible) — **confirmed.** No official DSP data on this event has ever been released; the sole public artifact is a leaked unclassified-presentation printout. DSP mission data remains classified.
- **C253** (wants DSP records to correlate with STS-48/STS-80) — **confirmed** as a statement of intent (Type A, transcript). No DSP data for those dates is public either; the STS incidents themselves are covered in a separate finding.

### Verdict challenges

- **C250** — quote-check #4 failed, so per audit protocol the verdict is marked "contested - audit flagged." Substantively the verdict's conclusion (attempted fire, no shot) is *not* in doubt — it rests on two independently re-verified anchors — but the finding overstated its own verification ("All quotes below were fetched and verified verbatim at the cited URL") for a paywalled source it could not have plainly fetched.
- **C249 ("unsupported, leaning debunked")** — challenged and upheld. Not too dismissive: the negative case comes from the printout's own discoverer (Sparks) with specific, checkable defects (date mismatch, no time/location in the unclassified artifact, undefined "SR" tag, single-satellite detection), and the finding fairly records the Regehr/Graham side as claims made. This is evidence-backed skepticism, not reflexive dismissal.
- **C252 ("confirmed")** — minor caution, no change. "Still classified / publicly inaccessible" is a negative-existence claim; the finding proves absence of any official release, which is the right evidence but inherently weaker than a positive confirmation. Verdict stands as the honest reading.
- **C246–C248, C251, C253** — checked against the presented evidence; no overclaim in either direction found. C248's support (a blogspot MUFON-lecture listing, an Art Bell archive entry, a Paracast forum post by Don Ecker) is the weakest sourcing in the file, but it is only asked to carry a Type-A "they made this claim" load, which it does.

### Citation-circle check

- The finding correctly self-identifies the DSP-detection chain as a single-root citation circle (one leaked printout → Regehr/Graham → podcast) — no complaint.
- **Watch-item, properly handled:** the skeptical section leans on Dunning, whose contractor-interview material is *derived from Klass*, not independent of him — the finding attributes this correctly ("Dunning, from Klass's contractor interviews") and does not present Klass+Dunning as two independent investigations. Similarly, the Medium/Kean/ufoquotes Jafari quotes all root to the single 2007 NPC statement, and the finding treats Jafari as a single-witness root rather than counting the outlets as corroboration. No hidden circles found presented as independence.
- The evaluation-form remarks are "verified" at two NICAP-hosted transcriptions (FUFOR summary + Clear Intent transcription) — same ecosystem, but this is transcription-checking of one document, not claimed corroboration. Acceptable.
